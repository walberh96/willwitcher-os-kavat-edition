# Other distros reopen eight Kavat decisions

The ideas that add the most value to Willwitcher-OS Kavat Edition are not desktop features but lifecycle mechanisms that the design does not have yet: user-state migrations (Omarchy), Arch manual interventions turned into code (Garuda and EndeavourOS), a health check that remembers the last good snapshot (openSUSE), prepared updates that are applied on reboot (`pacman-offline`), a Gamer mode tied to the life of the game's process (CachyOS) and a ladder of ephemeral sessions for Incognito (Kicksecure and Qubes). Before approving any of them, **eight findings force the reopening of decisions that the design document treats as closed**: Noctalia froze its Quickshell version and was rewritten in C++, Hyprland moved its configuration to Lua, mkinitcpio 42 invalidates TPM2 unlocking, Epic's next anti-cheat requires "recognized" Secure Boot keys, GRUB does not count failed boots, the AUR suffered two waves of malware that got past string-based scanners, Omarchy removed `/home` snapshots and secureblue advises against wrapping Chromium in a second sandbox. The catalog that follows gathers **54 new ideas in six areas, 36 refinements of pieces already decided and 17 things that are better not copied**, each with its origin, estimated cost and documented risk. The context that orders it all is that Omarchy 4.0 (August 2026) converged with Kavat's architecture—shell in Quickshell, everything in pacman packages, a wrapper over pacman—so its list of failures serves as a pre-mortem. Most of it is verified in a primary source read on 11 November 2026; what remains unverified, and is marked as such, is the requirements of EAC Linux v2, the closing of AUR pushes, GRUB's behavior with Btrfs and TPM2 and almost every gaming performance figure.

**Marks used throughout the report.** **[P]** = the researchers read it in a primary source (the project's own repository, wiki, manual or release notes). **[S]** = secondary source (press, forum, blog). **[NV]** = not verified: only a search-engine snippet was seen or the page could not be opened. **Cost** is an estimate by this synthesis for a single author, not a figure from the sources: **B** = days (configuration, one package or one script); **M** = weeks (a new component in `kav`, `kavatd` or the shell); **A** = months or ongoing maintenance. "Inference" marks a conclusion that no source states. Each idea carries a code so that it can be approved or discarded by it. "New" and "refinement" were decided against `docs/DESIGN.md` of 11 November 2026.

## Eight findings reopen decisions that the design treated as closed

The design document states that "all design decisions are made" and limits the reference to Omarchy to "only the idea of the global theme selector". Both sentences aged in 2026. Omarchy 4.0 "Quattro" (14 August) replaced Waybar, Walker, Mako, hyprlock and hypridle with a single Quickshell process and stopped being a git checkout to become pacman packages **[P]** ([Release v4.0.0](https://github.com/omacom/omarchy/releases/tag/v4.0.0)); it is now the project most similar to Kavat and, because of its volume of public issues, the one that offers the most documented failures (inference of this synthesis). Each finding cites the written decision it touches.

### The Noctalia fork no longer has an upstream in Quickshell

The design rests the desktop on "a fork of Noctalia (built on Quickshell)" maintained with "small patches on top of each upstream tag". That upstream ceased to exist in Quickshell. **Noctalia v5 was announced on 24 April 2026 as a from-scratch rewrite in C++ that abandons Qt**; v4 "is entering maintenance mode and its codebase will be frozen", QML plugins do not work in v5 and the latest version is 5.4.1 of 29 October **[P]** ([v5 announcement](https://noctalia.dev/blog/announcing-noctalia-v5), [Changelogs](https://noctalia.dev/changelogs)). The team justifies the change on the grounds that the Qt/QML stack used about 300 MB of RAM per monitor **[P]** ([v5 announcement](https://noctalia.dev/blog/announcing-noctalia-v5)). The blow reaches phase 1 ("ISO that boots into Hyprland + Noctalia"), the fork of phase 3 and plan B for the login, which was a greeter in Quickshell "that reuses Noctalia's code".

There are three ways out and the choice is the author's. The first is to adopt the frozen v4 as the project's own base and take on all of its maintenance. The second is to move to Noctalia v5, which supports Hyprland and isolates each Luau plugin in its own VM **[P]** ([Noctalia docs](https://docs.noctalia.dev/noctalia/)), but then the shell stops being Quickshell and Noctalia is only useful as a design, not as reusable QML. The third is to start from a live Quickshell project: Dux Shell (formerly DankMaterialShell, version 2.0 of 20 October 2026) **[P]** ([Dux Labs](https://duxlabs.dev/blog/v2-0-release)), Caelestia **[P]** ([repository](https://github.com/caelestia-dots/shell)), illogical-impulse **[P]** ([repository](https://github.com/end-4/dots-hyprland)) or Omarchy's shell, which has an MIT license **[P]** ([GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/omacom/omarchy)). That Omarchy is a candidate is an inference of this synthesis, and no note evaluates the code quality of those bases. HyprPanel, another common alternative, was archived on 27 April 2026 **[P]** ([HyprPanel](https://github.com/Jas-SinghFSU/HyprPanel)).

### Hyprland is configured in Lua since 0.55

"Since Hyprland 0.55, hyprlang is deprecated in favor of lua" **[P]** ([Hyprland 0.56 wiki](https://wiki.hypr.land/0.56.0/Configuring/Layouts/Scrolling-Layout/)); 0.55 came out in May 2026 according to a press summary **[NV]** ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2026/5/hyprland-0-55-adds-lua-configs-user-defined-layouts-improved-scrolling-and-icc-profiles/)). The design describes Hyprland's configuration as split files (`binds`, `rules`, `monitors`, `look`, `autostart`) and promises that the panel "edits that line in the file, without touching the rest or the comments". With Lua that file is code. Omarchy 4.0 has already migrated: `~/.config/hypr/hyprland.lua` loads the system's values and then the user's files, and `o.rebind` replaces a shortcut without touching Omarchy's file **[P]** ([manual/31-dotfiles.md](https://github.com/omacom/omarchy/blob/quattro/manual/31-dotfiles.md)); illogical-impulse maintains a "Pre-Hyprland Luaification" version for those who do not have 0.55 **[P]** ([end-4/dots-hyprland](https://github.com/end-4/dots-hyprland)).

Two consequences follow from this. The rule that the design already applies to `init.lua` and `.zshrc`—the panel only edits its own block—now applies to Hyprland too. And writing someone else's text into Lua is a route to code execution: Omarchy 4.0.1 had to publish "Stop USB device names from being executed as Hyprland Lua" **[P]** ([Release v4.0.1](https://github.com/omacom/omarchy/releases/tag/v4.0.1)). The cleanest way out (inference) is for the panel and the agent to write data—a table of values that the system's Lua loads—and never generate Lua from external strings.

### mkinitcpio 42 invalidates TPM2 unlocking until it is enrolled again

Arch announced on 22 September 2026 that **mkinitcpio 42 changes the measurements of PCRs 0-7, 9 and 12-14** by including `systemd-pcrosseparator.service` in the systemd hook; anyone who unlocks LUKS with a TPM2 bound to those PCRs must enroll it again **[P]** ([Arch News](https://archlinux.org/news/mkinitcpio-42-requires-manual-intervention-for-tpm2-based-unlocking-of-luks-devices/)). The design decides "LUKS2 with TPM2 unlocking and a recovery password" and a `kav update` that "reads the Arch news and stops". Stopping solves nothing: this is the textbook case for a coded intervention (idea A2) that detects the version, guides the re-enrollment and makes the post-update check verify the unlocking before rebooting.

The combination with GRUB also carries unverified assumptions. CachyOS documents that GRUB is the only one of its four boot loaders that encrypts `/boot`, but only with LUKS1 or PBKDF2 **[P]** ([Boot Managers](https://wiki.cachyos.org/installation/boot_managers/)). If the kernel of each snapshot lives inside the encrypted root, it is GRUB that must decrypt it, before Plymouth; if it lives outside, they no longer go "each with its own kernel". This is an inference of this synthesis and it reinforces an assumption that the design already lists for phase 2. The notes do not verify whether GRUB can use the TPM2 in that phase, nor the earlier belief that with GRUB the unlocking can only be bound to PCR 7, because predicting the kernel and initrd measurements presupposes a UKI **[NV]**. Two more facts for the decision: KDE Linux reported that systemd 259 "broke many TPM" chips **[P]** ([This Month in KDE Linux, March 2026](https://pointieststick.com/2026/03/31/this-month-in-kde-linux-march-2026/)), and secureblue adds a PIN or FIDO2 to the TPM2 **[P]** ([secureblue, features](https://secureblue.dev/features)).

### Epic's anti-cheat will ask for Secure Boot keys that a personal distro does not have

Epic announced on 14 October 2026 "EAC Linux v2": with no kernel module of its own, based on eBPF and BPF LSM, but with the kernel and modules signed by an organization whose key Epic recognizes, Secure Boot booting with that key, TPM 2.0, measured boot, IOMMU enabled, lockdown in integrity mode and kexec disabled. **Self-signed keys do not qualify by default**; the initial list includes those of Canonical, Fedora and Universal Blue, it will be tested first in Fortnite and current EAC games can stay on the old implementation **[S]** ([XenoSpectrum](https://xenospectrum.com/en/eac-linux-signed-kernel-requirements/)). The source is a translation: Epic's original post requires signing in and the system is not deployed yet.

The design decides "Secure Boot: own keys with `sbctl`". Kavat would meet the technical part and fail the trust part. The immediate impact is limited, because Fortnite is already listed as denied on Linux **[P]** ([Are We Anti-Cheat Yet?](https://areweanticheatyet.com/)), but the decision has to be made: accept that the titles that migrate to v2 are left out, or plan for an alternative boot entry with a kernel signed by a recognized third party. The second option is an inference from the notes and no source confirms that it is viable.

### GRUB does not count failed boots on its own

The design decides that "if the previous boot did not reach the desktop, the menu appears on its own with Safe mode already selected", and that from the shell you reboot "directly into safe mode, into a snapshot or into the firmware". systemd's attempt counting lives in systemd-boot: the counter goes in the file name of each entry, and any other boot loader would have to implement "the same rename logic" **[P]** ([Automatic Boot Assessment](https://systemd.io/AUTOMATIC_BOOT_ASSESSMENT/)). With classic GRUB each project writes its own: openSUSE stores in `/var/lib/misc/health-check.state` the last subvolume that booted well **[P]** ([openSUSE/health-checker](https://github.com/openSUSE/health-checker)) and Fedora uses variables in grubenv with patches specific to the distribution **[NV]** ([Fedora IoT issue #12](https://pagure.io/fedora-iot/issue/12)). No project for Arch turned up that reverts on its own after a failed boot with GRUB; what is documented is manual **[NV]** ([AUR, snapper-rollback](https://aur.archlinux.org/packages/snapper-rollback)).

Two unverified risks remain: that GRUB cannot write its environment block when `/boot` is on Btrfs, and that rebooting into a specific entry depends on an interface that systemd-boot implements and GRUB does not **[NV]**. What is verified works in favor of keeping the boot loader: GRUB is the only one of those that CachyOS compares that measures TPM PCRs, and Limine and rEFInd do not do so by default **[P]** ([Boot Managers](https://wiki.cachyos.org/installation/boot_managers/)). The recommendation is to keep GRUB, copy openSUSE's scheme (refinement R10), which covers failures in user space, and accept that a kernel that never gets to boot is only detected by a counter in the boot loader.

### The AUR of 2026 proves the allowlist right and dismantles string-based review

Arch warned on 12 June 2026 of "a high volume of malicious package adoptions and updates in the Arch User Repository" **[P]** ([Arch News](https://archlinux.org/news/active-aur-malicious-packages-incident/)). LWN counted **more than 1,500 affected packages, almost all of them orphans adopted by new accounts**; the PKGBUILD added an npm dependency and, from 13 June, Bun with the command obfuscated to evade string-based scanners; yay rejected that kind of scanning as "security theater" and switched to showing the last-modified date **[P]** ([LWN](https://lwn.net/Articles/1077619/)). A second wave led to blocking adoptions on 30 July and all pushes on 1 August **[S]** ([Gridinsoft](https://blog.gridinsoft.com/arch-aur-malware-openconnect-sso/)); it is not known whether the AUR has reopened.

The allowlist pinned to a commit neutralizes exactly that attack, a package that changes content without changing its name. What does not hold up is the heuristic of `kav review`, which flags "new domains, `curl | sh`, `.install` scripts, binaries without a checksum": it is the class of detection that the attack got past. Signals that do not depend on strings are needed: a change of maintainer since the reviewed commit, the last-modified date, new dependencies on `npm`, `bun` or `pip`, and building without network. Chaotic-AUR applies a list of trusted maintainers and sends to human review every change that goes beyond version and checksums **[P]** ([Garuda Wiki, AUR](https://wiki.garudalinux.org/en/maintenance/aur-packages/)). And if the AUR remains closed to pushes, the level 3 packages stay frozen at their last commit and keeping their PKGBUILDs in `[kavat]` gains weight (inference).

### Omarchy removed /home snapshots because of disk and CPU

The design promises `/home` snapshots "every hour, lightweight". Omarchy had them and **removed them in 3.6 because of excessive disk usage and because btrfs quotas consumed CPU** **[P]** ([Release v3.6.0](https://github.com/omacom/omarchy/releases/tag/v3.6.0)). KDE Linux went the opposite way in August 2026, with home folders in subvolumes, automatic snapshots and restoring from Dolphin, under the warning "This doesn't replace a backup, of course!" **[P]** ([This Month in KDE Linux, August 2026](https://blogs.kde.org/2026/08/31/this-month-in-kde-linux-august-2026/)). The idea survives; its naive version does not. It is better to do without btrfs quotas, shorten the retention and—inference—take Steam libraries, Wine prefixes, shader caches and Ollama models out of the snapshot, and add the case to the table of assumptions to verify.

### A second sandbox over Chromium can reduce security

The design includes "sandbox profiles for the browser and Electron apps". secureblue warns that its hardened Chromium must not be wrapped in Bubblejail, because an extra sandbox can clash with Chromium's internal one and reduce security, and recommends using Discord or Spotify as PWAs instead of their Electron apps **[P]** ([secureblue FAQ](https://secureblue.dev/faq)). A real example from 2026 that isolates a browser with `systemd-run --user` (home on tmpfs, selective mounts, no capabilities) admits that it isolates less than AppArmor or Firejail **[S]** ([CachyOS Forum](https://discuss.cachyos.org/t/hardening-browsers-with-systemd-run/32608)). The restriction of user namespaces, common in these profiles, also breaks the Steam Runtime container with "bwrap: Creating new namespace failed" **[S]** ([steam-runtime #486](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/steam-runtime/issues/486)). The browser profile has to be designed while checking that it does not degrade Chromium's own sandbox; for Electron, the cheap alternative is idea C4.

Two written decisions call for a minor adjustment. Incognito mode names "OpenVPN or Proton VPN", and Proton recommends WireGuard "whenever possible" and leaves OpenVPN for old devices **[P]** ([Proton VPN, Linux](https://protonvpn.com/support/linux-vpn-setup)). Gamer mode promises "maximum CPU performance", and CachyOS, which ships the wrapper that does just that, warns that it "generally gives no benefit, and may hurt" on CPUs below about 6 cores and 12 threads and does not enable it by default **[P]** ([cachyos-settings](https://wiki.cachyos.org/features/cachyos_settings/)). The design also describes Omarchy with old data: it says that there "a theme is a folder with one configuration file per app", and since 3.3 a theme is `colors.toml` plus templates **[P]** ([Release v3.3.0](https://github.com/omacom/omarchy/releases/tag/v3.3.0)); the difference that remains is that each Omarchy theme is a single variant, with no light/dark pair **[P]** ([manual/43](https://github.com/omacom/omarchy/blob/quattro/manual/43-making-your-own-theme.md)).

## The 54 new ideas: the cheap ones are in kav, Gamer mode and Incognito

These are the ideas that the design does not contain. Each table is preceded by this synthesis's position on what to approve first; the decision is the author's.

### Updates and the lifecycle of `kav`

The clearest gap in the design is here. It takes a snapshot before each transaction, but nothing changes the state that pacman does not own, and faced with a manual intervention it stops instead of resolving it. I would approve A1 and A2 first—with the state of the migrations stored inside the root, so as not to repeat Omarchy's failure—and A8; A3 and A4 reinforce each other and deserve to be decided together.

### Recovery and boot

What is new here is little and cheap; I would approve B1 in its minimal version (the base snapshot) and B4.

### Declarative system, software and development

The author comes from NixOS with a flake, and what fits from that world into a mutable Arch is the workflow, not the formal guarantee. C1 only pays off together with A3, because pacman does not pin versions if the repository is not dated. C4 and C5 cost almost nothing with a browser and a launcher of its own.

## What was verified in a primary source and what was not

What is solid is what comes from official code and documentation read on 11 November 2026. Omarchy was read by cloning its repository (the `quattro` branch, which is the development one, so some detail in `docs/` may be later than the stable 4.5.0) and its release notes. The Garuda and EndeavourOS updaters were read in the script itself, not in its description. Also primary are the Arch news, CachyOS's policy and wiki, the systemd manual pages, openSUSE's `health-checker`, the documentation of secureblue, Kicksecure, Qubes, Tails and GrapheneOS, the announcements of Noctalia, Plasma, GNOME and elementary, the Hyprland 0.56 wiki and the READMEs of LACT, MangoHud, gamescope and gpu-screen-recorder. The eight findings of the first section rest on a primary source except where marked otherwise.

What is not verified affects specific decisions and should be kept in mind when approving. The requirements of EAC Linux v2 come from a secondary translation and the system is not deployed. The closing of AUR pushes on 1 August is reported by a third-party blog and the current status is unknown. Everything about GRUB beyond the systemd document—writing grubenv on Btrfs, TPM2 with PCR prediction, rebooting into an entry, the `grub-btrfs-overlayfs` hook—was left without a source, because the Arch Wiki blocked access during the whole investigation; for the same reason nothing is verified about HDR on Hyprland or about the Gaming, Snapper or `systemd-cryptenroll` pages. Phoronix returned 403, so the CachyOS performance figures are summaries, and no independent 2026 benchmark of sched-ext schedulers in games was found, nor any measurements of the fps cost of kernel hardening. The current mechanism of Proton's kill switch on Linux was only seen in snippets.

There are also limits of method. The evidence on which desktop features are used daily is anecdotal: no Reddit thread was retrieved and no public telemetry exists, so the separation between useful and ornament is tentative. Of Omarchy's issues only the title, status and votes were read. Omarchy's documentation contradicts itself about the migration markers of a new user, and a cached copy of Noctalia's README contradicts its documentation about the status of v5 plugins. Neither the maintenance of aconfmgr nor the current behavior of `nix shell` was confirmed. The update mechanisms of SteamOS and KDE Linux were not investigated from a primary source, nor Whonix, nor other Arch derivatives. And two columns of this report are judgment and not data: the adoption cost and every sentence marked as inference.

## Conclusion

The common thread of the others' failures is not bad engineering but the state that lives outside what gets reverted. Omarchy's migration markers survive in `/home` a rollback of the root; Bazzite needed manual steps to go back a version because session management had changed; KDE Linux had to plan to move `/etc` along with each image; CachyOS warns that with Limine you cannot go back to a snapshot if a kernel update happened in between. Kavat will have at least four stores of state—root snapshots, `/home` snapshots, git history of the configuration and the allowlist—and no source formulates the rule that all those incidents point to: each new mechanism must declare which snapshot its state belongs to and what happens to it when that snapshot is restored. Applied from the start, that rule is worth more than any of the 54 ideas.

The order of approval matters more than the number. The shell base and Hyprland's Lua format change what is packaged in phase 1 and must be resolved before writing code; the `kav` mechanisms (A1, A2, R10) are cheap while `kav` does not exist and expensive to graft on later; and in Gamer mode it is better to measure first (E5) and tune afterwards, because almost nothing that the gaming distros promise has a benchmark. One implication remains that the design did not foresee: with EAC Linux v2, the security posture stops being only a security decision and becomes a compatibility requirement, so the Secure Boot keys are now also a decision about which games will work. Five new assumptions deserve to enter the design's "to verify" table: that GRUB decrypts LUKS2 and boots each snapshot with its kernel without asking for two passwords, that automatic safe mode has a real mechanism with GRUB on Btrfs, that TPM2 unlocking survives a mkinitcpio update, that hourly `/home` snapshots do not degrade disk or CPU, and that the browser's sandbox does not weaken Chromium's.
